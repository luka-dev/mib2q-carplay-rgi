# DSD100 Independent-Corpus Acquisition

_Track B, 2026-09-29. Acquisition and validation boundary; no model or audio result yet._

## Why this corpus

All 40 MoisesDB DEV songs and 20 of the former TEST songs have been inspected during model and renderer selection. The owner-library teacher stems are estimates, so they cannot provide an independent real-stem product check. The [SigSep DSD100 release](https://sigsep.github.io/datasets/dsd100.html) lists **100 full-length stereo songs**, split into **50 train and 50 test**, with isolated vocals, drums, bass and `other`; its mixture is the sum of the sources at **44.1 kHz**. Its official direct archive reports **15,989,815,305 bytes** and supports byte-range resume. This is a useful new natural-song source/protection/stability set, provided song identities are checked for overlap before use.

Reserve all **50 DSD100 TEST** songs for an evaluation locked before any result inspection. The DSD100 TRAIN half can later be considered as another real-stem training pool only in a separate matched intervention. DSD100's `other` is a combined source: it does **not** supply separate guitar, keys, piano or strings truth and cannot alone certify those family thresholds. The test half's artist and song overlap with MoisesDB, the owner's library, AlbumDB and any earlier MUSDB18-derived training must be audited from metadata before calling it independent. Older Track A models used MUSDB18, which includes DSD100 material, so independence is **model-specific**, not automatic.

## Remaining independent instrument coverage

DSD100's four-way `other` cannot independently score guitar, keys, piano and strings. The [MedleyDB release page](https://medleydb.weebly.com/downloads.html) offers instrument-level multitracks, but its full audio requires a Zenodo access request and is restricted to non-commercial research. The [MS500 project repository](https://github.com/felixCheungcheung/mixing_secrets_v2) documents a larger instrument-labelled collection built from Cambridge multitracks, but its README still says the packaged Zenodo release is pending. The owner has authorized a separate [three-song Cambridge training trial](UPMIX_CAMBRIDGE_TRIAL_2026-09-29.md); it is a tiny TRAIN pool, not an untouched instrument-family evaluation set. The [Cambridge FAQ](https://www.cambridge-mt.com/ms3/mtk-faq/) requires individual licensing for AI training, and the owner says that permission was obtained; its record is still to be added to the repository. An external full product claim remains open until at least 20 sufficiently covered, untouched real-stem songs pass the frozen renderer and source tests.

## Prepared Dev-only training cache and matched comparison

The new [`cache_dsd100_dev.py`](../../tools/stereo-upmix-render/cache_dsd100_dev.py) reads only `Sources/Dev` and `Mixtures/Dev` from a fully validated archive. It maps the four real source groups to the existing seven-slot 48 kHz int16 cache: vocals → lead vocal, drums → drum core, bass → bass, `other` → eligible other; the unused slots are zero. It rejects missing roles, duplicate numeric IDs, tracks shorter than 60 s, stem-length spread over 150 ms and a first-60-s mastered-mix residual above −20 dB. The mastered WAV is an alignment/closure audit, never a label. The training mixture remains the exact sum of the cached sources. It publishes `split.json` with **50 TRAIN** entries only after all songs finish, and supports a checked resume. A synthetic 60-s four-stem ZIP produced a seven-slot `(7, 2, 2,880,000)` int16 cache with a −64.6 dB master residual; a separate 50-track synthetic manifest passed the Dev-only parser. Neither test uses the real archive.
