  front stage. Words versus wordless samples, stereo width, reverb and a separator
  filename do not determine musical role. Ambiguous vocal material stays front.
- Backing vocals may be partially added to the rear only when role AND lack of
  lead contamination are supported. Rear permission is optional, not an obligation
  to empty the front backing track. A `backing`/karaoke-residual filename is not a label.
- Lead-vocal reverb, vocal chops and call-and-response are protected until their
  role is established. Cities In Dust around 103 s is a named ambiguity regression,
  not an automatically approved backing-positive example.
- Preserve lead melody, bass/kick foundation, instrument body and front image.
  Do not solve vocal leakage by globally muting the rear, rolling away all mids,
  lowering the whole song, or sending only hiss/cymbal air to tweeters.
- PCM L/R/C and physical front speakers are different domains. Initial stereo
  rendering retains PCM C=0: the recovered J525 matrix already forms a center
  feed from L/R. Do not duplicate a lead onto PCM C without an independently
  verified front/center rendering design. No unconstrained inverse matrix.
- Distinguish zero **added digital** lead in Ls/Rs from physical rear audibility
  after stock DSP. 3D OFF is not bypass. Unknown downstream processing and cabin
  acoustics cannot be certified by a static input-matrix simulation.

### Genre-agnostic coverage and electronic-music stress contract

This is a required design/acceptance extension, not evidence that the existing
dataset or G17 already covers these cases. Do not alter an active frozen run,
relabel its targets or open final-heldout audio to satisfy the new requirement.

- Foreground is not synonymous with singing. A main growl/wobble bass riff,
  distorted synth, guitar lead, sampled phrase or wordless vocal hook remains a
  protected front anchor when it carries the foreground part. Frequency range,
  stereo width, distortion and source-family name cannot grant rear permission.
  In particular, do not detach a foreground bass sound's upper harmonics and
  send them rearward merely because they occupy mid/high frequencies.
- Supporting synth/guitar/string parts, verified backing, percussion and FX may
  contribute behind the listener when their actual role and purity support it.
  A riser, impact, reverb tail or high-frequency event is not automatically
  background. Preserve useful body and original musical motion; do not replace
  the rear stage with only treble, a synthetic constant bed or an energy quota.
- Audit TRAIN/DEV coverage by audible production characteristics as well as
  source metadata: heavy distortion, dense/unison overlap, extreme stereo,
  mono/near-mono mixes, sidechain amplitude modulation, compressed/limited
  material, sparse acoustic passages, and sudden section/foreground changes.
  Include electronic and non-electronic material. Genre tags may help coverage
  audits and reporting but must not become placement permission or a preset.
- Retain natural mixes and time-varying per-source role evidence. Document gaps
  before adding bounded, provenance-preserving TRAIN material; do not infer
  genre coverage from song counts or turn ambiguous sources into rear positives.
- Evaluate full transitions (including build/drop/breakdown) and longer excerpts:
  front-anchor retention, useful rear recovery, attacks/tails, stereo stability,
  control-induced pumping, bass/headroom and prompt revocation of stale roles.
  Do not smooth away intended modulation to improve a stability score. Report
  per-song and worst-condition results; absent evidence remains unqualified.
  Genre-agnostic design alone is not demonstrated cross-genre generalization.
